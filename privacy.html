Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 6, 2026

Stadiate is built to be self-hosted and local-first. It runs on hardware you control, inside your own home network. We do not operate a cloud service that collects your data, and Stadiate has no user accounts.

The short version: Stadiate does not collect, sell, rent, or share your personal information. Your configuration, your smart-home credentials, and your activity stay on your own device.

Information we do not collect

Stadiate does not require you to create an account and does not transmit personal information to us. Specifically, we do not collect your name, email, location, contacts, advertising identifiers, or usage analytics.

Information stored on your device

To do its job, Stadiate stores the following locally, on the hardware you run it on:

  • Your settings — the teams and leagues you follow, your automation rules, and screen layouts.
  • Device credentials — the keys and addresses needed to control your own smart-home gear (for example a Philips Hue bridge, Sonos speakers, Govee lights, or Home Assistant). These are used only to talk to those devices on your network.
  • A local PIN — an optional PIN you set to protect access to your hub.

This information never leaves your network unless you explicitly export it yourself.

Services Stadiate connects to

Stadiate makes requests to a small number of outside services, always from your own device and never by way of a server we run. We receive nothing from any of them. Each is listed here so you can see the whole picture:

  • ESPN — publicly available scores, schedules, rosters and play-by-play for the games you follow, plus team and player images. This is the only one that is always on; it is how Stadiate knows a touchdown happened.
  • Philips Hue — if you enable Hue lighting without specifying your bridge's address, Stadiate asks Philips' discovery service which bridges are visible from your network so it can find yours. That request reveals your public IP address to Philips, as any web request does. Set the bridge address yourself and the request is never made.
  • Sleeper — only if you turn on fantasy features and give Stadiate your Sleeper username, which it sends to Sleeper's public API to read your league and roster. Off unless you enable it.
  • ElevenLabs — only if you choose it as your announcer voice and supply your own API key. The line to be spoken is sent to ElevenLabs to be turned into audio. Stadiate's default announcer runs entirely on your own machine and sends nothing anywhere.
  • Yahoo Finance — only if you switch on the optional market ticker, which fetches quotes for the symbols you list. Off by default.

Everything after the first item is something you turn on deliberately. Your use of these services is governed by their own privacy policies.

Smart-home and network devices

When you connect lights, speakers, a TV board, or a home-automation hub, Stadiate communicates with those devices directly over your local network to trigger the effects you configure. Your smart-home credentials stay on your own hardware — they are never sent to us, and never to any third party. The one request that leaves your network is the optional Philips bridge lookup described above, which carries no credentials and no device data.

Children's privacy

Stadiate is a general-audience sports companion app and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Data security

Because Stadiate is self-hosted, the security of your data depends primarily on your own network and device. Stadiate stores device credentials locally and supports an optional PIN lock. We recommend running Stadiate on a trusted home network.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as Stadiate evolves. Material changes will be reflected on this page with a new "last updated" date.
