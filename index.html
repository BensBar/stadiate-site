Stadiate in plain English

It’s an app for your TV that shows your team’s game as a beautiful live scoreboard. If you want more, it can also flash your lights and play the goal horn when your team scores.

1

Put it on your TV

Open Stadiate on your Apple TV and pick your teams. Your TV becomes a live scoreboard, or a piece of art that shows the score.

Needs: Apple TV
2

Connect your room (optional)

Run the Stadiate Mac server on a Mac. It links Stadiate to your smart lights and speakers, so the room can join in.

Needs: a Mac + smart lights or speakers
3

Your team scores. Everything reacts.

The lights flash your team’s colors, the horn sounds, and a framed art screen on another wall lights up with your touchdown. Your main game is never interrupted. That’s what we mean by feel the game.

Lights and sound need step 2

NOSPOILERS

Big reactions. Not big spoilers.

The celebration should never
beat the play to your screen.

Live sports data usually arrives a few seconds ahead of your stream. Stadiate holds each moment, then lets you line it up with your TV using SYNC on your phone. The default hold is about 75 seconds; adjust it to match your broadcast.

Level 1 · The simple setup

Just Apple TV.

One app. A whole new way to watch.

Stadiate gets sports data directly on your Apple TV. No Mac running in the background and nothing else to set up.

  • Live scores, team follows, and multiview boards
  • Arena, full-screen ticker, and 14 Art View themes
  • On-screen celebrations and native horn sounds
  • Anti-spoiler delay and a paired phone remote
Bring: Apple TV, an internet connection, and a phone on the same Wi-Fi for the remote.

Keep Stadiate open on the Apple TV. tvOS does not keep it polling or serving its remote in the background.

Level 2 · The whole-room setup

Add the Mac server.

From a great screen to a great room.

Run the Stadiate hub on a Mac that stays on during games. Pair your Apple TV, or open browser boards on other screens.

  • Hue, Govee LAN, Home Assistant, and Sonos
  • Custom rules, sound effects, and spoken calls
  • Multiple boards, per-screen layouts, shared takeovers
  • Fantasy-player scoring celebrations and roster selection
  • Live video capture, picture-in-picture, and webhooks
Bring: An awake Mac on your network. Add compatible lights, speakers, or capture hardware for those features.

The Mac handles the room, even when Stadiate isn’t the active app on an Apple TV. That TV must still have Stadiate open to display its board.

Before kickoff

Good questions.
Straight answers.

So what actually is Stadiate?+

Stadiate is an app for Apple TV that shows live sports scores as a big, beautiful scoreboard or as living art. If you add the optional Mac server, it can also flash your smart lights, play sounds, and light up a second screen (like a framed art TV) when your team scores, while your main game keeps playing. It doesn’t stream the game itself.

What is the “Mac server”?+

A small Stadiate program that runs on a Mac while you watch. It acts as the go-between for your lights, speakers, and extra screens, which is why it’s needed for room effects. You can skip it and still use Stadiate on your Apple TV.

Can I celebrate my fantasy football players?+

Yes, with the Mac server. Use Sleeper, a manual roster, or the experimental CBS connector, choose starters only or your whole roster, and check that your players are mapped. NFL fantasy players can be watched even when their teams aren’t in Follows. Their scoring events still pass through the anti-spoiler delay and per-game SYNC. This is player-score detection and celebration, not a fantasy-points or league-standings service. CBS is best-effort, not an official public API, and its embedded sign-in may be blocked. The safe preview is a labeled demonstration; it does not trigger real lights or sounds.

Does Stadiate stream the actual game?+

No. Standalone boards visualize sports data; Stadiate does not include broadcast rights, TV channels, or a sports subscription. With the Mac server, you can bring your own supported video source through screen or USB capture. That requires ffmpeg, the relevant macOS permissions, and an unprotected source; DRM restrictions still apply.

Can it run alongside another app on one Apple TV?+

Not as an overlay on another tvOS app. Stadiate needs to be the foreground app to show its board, poll scores, and serve its phone remote. Use it on a companion screen beside your broadcast, or use the Mac server’s supported capture setup to bring video into a Stadiate board.

Do Art View and the phone remote need a Mac?+

No. Art View gets its sports data on the Apple TV and includes its artwork in the app. For the standalone phone remote, open Settings → Phone Remote on the TV and scan the QR code with a phone on the same Wi-Fi. The pairing code protects control commands.

Do I need smart lights or special hardware?+

Not for standalone Apple TV boards, Art View, ticker, or native celebrations. For room automation, add the Mac server and compatible gear: a Hue bridge and lights, Govee devices with LAN Control enabled, Sonos, or devices managed by Home Assistant. A capture device is only needed if you choose the USB video-capture route.

Is Stadiate local-first?+

Yes. Stadiate runs on your hardware and does not require a Stadiate cloud account. An internet connection is still needed to fetch sports data. Optional integrations may have their own accounts, services, or requirements. Read the privacy policy for more.
